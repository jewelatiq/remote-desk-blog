Why Remote Workers Need a VPN on Public WiFi (2026 Security Guide)

July 30, 2026 2 min read Home Office Security

Disclosure: This post may contain affiliate links, including for NordVPN. If you sign up through one, I may earn a commission at no extra cost to you.

You grab your laptop, head to the nearest coffee shop, connect to "Free_Cafe_WiFi," and get to work. It feels normal — millions of remote workers do this every day. But that "free" WiFi network is one of the easiest ways for someone to see exactly what you're doing online.

If your job involves client data, company logins, invoices, or anything you wouldn't want a stranger reading over your shoulder, this guide is for you.

Public networks — at cafes, airports, hotels, and co-working spaces — are usually unencrypted or use shared passwords that hundreds of people know. That opens the door to a few common risks: packet sniffing, fake hotspots (evil twin attacks), session hijacking, and man-in-the-middle attacks. None of this requires advanced hacking skills — cheap, widely available tools make it possible for almost anyone to try.

A VPN (Virtual Private Network) creates an encrypted tunnel between your device and the internet. Even if someone is monitoring the same public network, all they see is scrambled data — not your passwords, messages, or files. For remote workers, this means encrypted connections on any WiFi, a hidden IP address, protected file transfers, and safer access to work platforms while traveling.

Freelancers working from cafes and co-working spaces, remote employees logging into company systems from outside the office, anyone handling client contracts or payment details on the go, and digital nomads switching networks frequently — all of these fall into the group that benefits most from running a VPN by default.

When picking a VPN for work rather than just streaming, a few things matter more than flashy marketing: a no-logs policy, strong encryption (AES-256 is the current baseline), a kill switch so you're never exposed if the VPN drops, real speed for video calls and file uploads, and support across multiple devices. This is where NordVPN fits well for remote workers — it covers all of the above, and it's one of the more established names in the space.

The habit worth building is simple: before you open your laptop on any network that isn't your home WiFi, turn the VPN on first. Most VPN apps connect in one tap and run quietly in the background — it costs you nothing in convenience once it's set up.

Public WiFi isn't going away, and neither is remote work. The two together just mean a little extra care is worth it, especially when client trust and your own accounts are on the line. A VPN is one of the cheapest, lowest-effort protections you can add to your remote work setup.
