Why Remote Workers Need a VPN (And How to Actually Choose One)

July 18, 2026 1 min read Security Remote Work

Disclosure: This post may contain affiliate links, including for NordVPN. If you sign up through one, I may earn a commission at no extra cost to you.

If you've ever sent a client invoice, logged into a work account, or shared a document while sitting in a coffee shop, you've used a public network that anyone else in that building could potentially snoop on. Most remote workers never think about this until something goes wrong.

A VPN encrypts your internet connection, so even on an unsecured public Wi-Fi network, the data flowing between your device and the internet is scrambled and unreadable to anyone else on that network. This matters more the more client data, financial information, or business documents you handle daily.

Beyond public Wi-Fi, a VPN also hides your browsing activity from your home internet provider, which is relevant if you value privacy or occasionally need to access work resources that are region-locked.

When choosing a VPN, three things actually matter: a strict no-logs policy (meaning the company itself can't hand over your browsing history even if asked), a kill switch (which cuts your internet if the VPN connection drops, so you're never accidentally exposed), and server speed (a slow VPN will frustrate you into turning it off, which defeats the purpose).

NordVPN is one of the most established options in this space, with a long-standing no-logs policy, dedicated servers in dozens of countries, and consistent performance for video calls and file uploads.

You don't need a VPN running every second of every day. But for anyone doing client work from shared spaces, hotels, or unfamiliar networks, it's one of the cheapest forms of insurance available.
