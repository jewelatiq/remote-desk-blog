NordVPN vs ExpressVPN vs Surfshark: Which One Actually Fits Remote Workers?

July 20, 2026 2 min read Security Remote Work

Disclosure: This post contains affiliate links, including for NordVPN. If you sign up through one, I may earn a commission at no extra cost to you.

If you've searched for a VPN recently, you've probably seen these three names everywhere. All three are legitimate, well-established services with strong no-logs policies, so the real question isn't which one is 'best' in the abstract — it's which one fits how you actually work.

For remote workers specifically, three things matter more than marketing claims: how reliably the connection holds during video calls, how many devices you can cover under one plan, and how straightforward the app is when you're switching networks constantly between home, cafés, and co-working spaces.

NordVPN tends to stand out for consistency — connection drops are rare, and its server network is large enough that you're rarely far from a fast one, which matters most during client calls. It also covers up to 10 devices on most plans, useful if you're juggling a laptop, phone, and tablet.

ExpressVPN has a reputation for slightly better raw speed in some regions, and its interface is arguably the simplest of the three. It tends to be priced a bit higher, which matters if you're budget-conscious as a freelancer.

Surfshark's biggest advantage is unlimited device connections on a single plan, which is genuinely useful if you share the subscription with family or manage several personal devices alongside your work laptop.

For most remote workers whose main need is stable connections during calls and reliable protection on public Wi-Fi, NordVPN's combination of consistency and device coverage tends to be the easiest to recommend without reservation.

Whichever you choose, the most important habit is simply turning it on before connecting to any network you don't fully control — that single habit prevents more problems than picking the 'perfect' provider ever will.
